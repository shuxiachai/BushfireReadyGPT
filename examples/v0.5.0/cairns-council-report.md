## 6. Local Risk Context
### Bushfire
- Queensland bushfire planning should account for official warning systems, changing fire weather, and local disaster coordination.
- Preparedness plans should separate routine readiness actions from active emergency decisions.
- Current fire danger ratings, fire bans, warnings, and evacuation advice must be verified through official Queensland channels.

### Smoke and Health Risks
- Cairns planning should consider tropical heat, smoke exposure, heavy rain or severe weather interactions, and road disruption.
- Power and communications outages can affect alerts, refrigeration, lighting, welfare checks, and access to official warning information.
- Surrounding vegetation, access roads, visitors, temporary residents, and seasonal population changes should be considered in communication planning.
- Community plans should define who checks on high-risk residents and how updates are distributed if internet or power fails.

### Road and Power
- Road closures and power outages can significantly impact evacuation and communication.
- Local council information and state emergency warnings should be used together.

### Community Vulnerability
- The population of Cairns is 172,888, with 15.6% of the population being older people.
- No-car households and language support needs are medium.
- Plain-language and multilingual communication should be considered for warnings, drills, and parent/community updates.
