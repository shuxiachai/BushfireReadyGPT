## 6. Local Risk Context
The local risk context includes bushfire, smoke, heat, road, power, communications, and community vulnerability considerations. The Queensland Fire Department, Bureau of Meteorology, and Get Ready Queensland provide official warning systems and information. The plan must consider tropical heat, smoke exposure, heavy rain, severe weather interactions, and road disruption. Power and communications outages can affect alerts, refrigeration, lighting, welfare checks, and access to official warning information. Community plans should include vulnerable residents, transport support, multilingual communication, pets, and household readiness.

## 8. Evacuation Planning
The warning monitoring process involves checking official Queensland channels for current bushfire incidents, fire bans, and warnings. Notification processes include using primary and backup channels for staff, students, families, and official updates. Movement processes involve identifying safe evacuation routes and roll-call responsibilities. Accountability and update processes include regular checks and updates from responsible officers.

## 9. Candidate Assembly Point Criteria
Criteria for candidate assembly points include:
- Secure accommodation further inland on higher ground
- Easy access for all community members
- Adequate facilities and resources
- Local approval required before treating as safe

Internal communication involves regular updates from responsible officers. Public/parent communication includes using official channels for updates. Multilingual needs are addressed through plain-language and multilingual communication. Backup channels include alternative communication methods in case of power outages.
